# RealtyCook: competitive landscape

Scope: Indian residential and commercial real estate for buyers, sellers and investors, including NRIs. The analysis is qualitative and was put together from public positioning, not from traffic or revenue data. Check figures before using them in pitches.

## Who RealtyCook competes with

| Segment | Representative players | What they do well | Where they fall short |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listing portals | 99acres, MagicBricks, Housing.com | Huge inventory, search filters, price trends, SEO | Unverified or duplicate listings, heavy ads, leads sold to many brokers, no advice |
| Broker-free platforms | NoBroker | Owner-to-tenant/buyer matching, paid add-ons (legal, packers, loans) | Mostly rental-led, self-serve, limited help on investment-grade or commercial deals |
| Full-stack brokerages | Square Yards, PropTiger, Anarock (retail) | New-launch inventory from developers, NRI desks, loans, home-loan tie-ups | Developer-funded, so they push new launches; resale and selling help is weaker |
| Fractional ownership / SM REIT platforms | Strata, hBits, PropertyShare | Lower ticket size for Grade-A commercial, rental payouts, digital onboarding | One asset class, lock-ins and exit liquidity are not obvious to retail investors |
| Listed REITs | Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, Nexus | Liquid, regulated, diversified | Market-linked, no control or choice of asset |
| Local brokers | Neighbourhood agents | Hyper-local knowledge, relationships, quick site visits | Opaque commissions, inconsistent paperwork, earn on closing rather than fit |

## Patterns in competitor websites

- **Search-first homepages.** A big search bar, city picker and endless cards. Good for browsing, but there's no guidance for someone who doesn't yet know *what* to buy.
- **Lead capture everywhere.** Pop-ups and "get callback" forms that pass leads to many agents. Users talk about being spammed with calls, so trust is low.
- **Flat, stock-photo visuals.** Few portals use 3D or interactive storytelling beyond virtual tours of individual listings.
- **Siloed journeys.** Buy, sell, rent and invest are separate products or sites. Nobody positions the *portfolio* as one plan.
- **Investment content is thin.** Yield, lease tenure, exit and tax are rarely explained on the pages that sell investments.

## Gaps RealtyCook can own

1. **One advisor across buy, sell and invest.** The site's core promise is one kitchen and every kind of deal, built around a single named advisor.
2. **Verification before discovery.** RERA, title and approval checks happen *before* site visits, the opposite of portal behaviour.
3. **Investment literacy.** An interactive returns calculator, plain-English yield and exit notes, and fractional ownership explained without jargon.
4. **Privacy as a feature.** "Your number is never shared with builders" directly answers portal spam.
5. **Memorable brand world.** The *cook / recipe* metaphor (ingredients, prep, serve) plus a playful 3D city on a serving platter makes RealtyCook look different from the listing grids everyone else uses.

## How the landing page uses this

| Insight | Landing page response |
|---|---|
| Users don't know where to start | Hero "I want to: Buy / Sell / Invest / Rent" intent picker that pre-fills the enquiry |
| Siloed services | "Six ways to deal" grid: buy, sell, invest, fractional, plots, NRI |
| Lack of process transparency | "The RealtyCook recipe": four numbered steps |
| Thin investment content | 3D returns calculator with an honest disclaimer |
| Low trust in portals and brokers | Dark comparison band: portals vs brokers vs RealtyCook |
| Call spam | Contact panel promises one advisor call and no sharing with builders |

## Open questions for the brand

- Primary cities and micro-markets (copy currently names Gurugram, Noida, Pune, Bengaluru and Mumbai as examples).
- Real proof points: deals closed, value transacted, years in business, RERA agent registration number.
- Fee model (flat fee, percentage, developer-paid) so the "fees in writing" promise can be specific.
- Whether fractional ownership is offered through partners, and which ones.
